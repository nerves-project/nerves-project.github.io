Goatmire Elixir aka. NervesConf EU

Many words have been written about Goatmire Elixir already. This year we didn't separately brand the NervesConf EU day because that's just painful. But it was very much a celebration of Nerves and all things embedded in Elixir.

This year's badge was an ESP32 running AtomVM actually. This was driven by curiosity as well as cost. I don't know if you've seen memory costs recently but just the memory of last year's badge would have cost more than the total BOM that year. This year was also a step up in many capabilities. Keyboard, a fast color display, more connectivity, more sensors. People had a blast with this thing.

So let's back up a little.

We did workshops for two days prior to the conference starting properly. All the hardware workshops were completely full. They also ran out of coffee first, consistently.

  • Nerves Community Hack Session - bit of an unconference type thing
  • Hardware Hacking - Gus leading a session on the new badge
  • AtomVM is Real - Mostly more badge hacking, but teaching AtomVM
  • Nerves as your smartphone OS - Everyone handed a Fairphone 3 running Nerves
  • Achieving balance in the workshop - Balance robot with BEAM Bots

Workshops were not recorded. If you want to see more of these workshops. Talk to the people who gave them and talk to event organizers like Code Sync and the Tates (for Gig City). These were free workshops held at a university campus. The people running them put in tremendous work for 4 hours of intense fun and learning.

Then we had a full day of the hardware-centric part of the Goatmire conference. With Nerves-powered instruments in the theater, a Nerves-powered 900 Neopixel tree and a lot of silliness. Then the talks, which were a good mix of out there and practical.

All talks from Goatmire will be released when we get through the editing process. We can't wait either.

My full write-up is on the blog, titled Goatmire Elixir 2 - Odin's Folly . Ellyse shared a lovely post with her personal journey through the conference. And Ellie Fairholm of BEAMOps wrote a lovely post on their site. And Davide Bettio wrote about the badge and his Goatmire experience in depth with a focus on AtomVM.

Ripples across the discords

As you know by now we have a Discord. You should join it at nerv.es/discord .

Goatmire also had a Discord and piece by piece we've been moving the hardware conversations from Goatmire over to the all-year-around Nerves Discord.

And just a side-note. Making a Discord for Nerves has been a massive boon. It is more alive than any of our past channels. The attention goes into one place. People know where to share, ask and collaborate. I still monitor most other channels and funnel folks towards the Discord. It is a lovely spot to hang out.

Since Goatmire it has been on a tear with people putting Nerves on smartphones left and right. Some on their own, some using Marc Lainez' tooling. I personally bought the same Android watch that Marc used for his talk at NervesConf EU and got up and running with it, with some help. And am now streaming video and doing GPU acceleration on a smartwatch, it is stupid amounts of fun.

The Fairphone 3 devices that people got as part of the workshop are all getting new experimental OS:es.

Damir's Emerge UI framework is now running on so many new devices.

My experimentation also involved shoving Ben Bangert's (experimental) universal_proxy onto a Pi and using the HDMI audio out to do Sendspin and Music Assistant in my home on with my home theatre receiver. The Home Assistant-supporting folks over at Nabu Casa sniffing around Nerves is a delight. They do Elixir for the cloud stuff already and I do hope the experimental Home Assistant clustering implementation vagus ends up going mainstream.

What is very interesting on the DIY side is that the libraries in use for universal_proxy just enable very smooth onboarding of devices into Home Assistant via improv . I mostly use homex for my devices so far but you also have the espex library for making your Nerves device do ESPHome.

We've seen people improvise balance bots from the Fairphone. We've seen ancient HTC Desire phones get Nerves on truly old Linux versions. And on the SHARP Brain PW-SH6 ( blog post in Japanese is worth a translation) which is an adorable device. Nerves system here .

Suffice it to say. Things are fun :)

Software updates

In the middle of all the fun we also ship new Nerves software for all your serious and unserious users out there. Since I've been shirking my newsletter duties this will be a big one.

These updates of course include the critical CA updates to ditch eMudhra after the number 4 was discovered to be a vulnerability.

nerves, v1.15.1
  • Raise the HTTP response header limit to 64 KiB so artifact downloads work with servers that return large headers. (@georgeguimaraes)
  • Use TLS 1.2 for artifact downloads to avoid connection resets on networks with middleboxes that don’t support TLS 1.3.
  • Report a missing ERTS_DIR environment variable explicitly instead of passing nil to Mix and causing a cryptic release configuration error.  (@LostKobrakai)

nerves, v2.0.0-pre.4

  • Fix typo preventing SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH from propogating through the firmware creation step. (@jannikbecher)

nerves, v2.0.0-pre.3
  • Don’t require TARGET_CPU in Nerves systems since this isn’t needed for x86_64.

System updates

Note: all the official nerves systems got updates for nerves_system_br v1.35.2 and v1.35.3. So the three releases v1.35.0 -> v1.35.2 end up producing a single set of updated systems. And then another set for v1.35.3. So that's what you'll encounter below.

nerves_system_br, v1.35.0

This is a major Buildroot update to 2026.08.

  • Changes

    • Fix regression where the Erlang available memory patch was not being applied to Erlang/OTP 29. Without this patch, the system_memory_high_watermark alarm would be incorrectly set. The patch was originally added in v1.31.4. Earlier Erlang/OTP versions continued to have the patch.

  • Package updates

    • Buildroot 2026.08
    • Erlang/OTP 29.1

nerves_system_br, v1.35.1

This is a security and bug fix release.

  • Package updates
    • Erlang/OTP 29.1.1

nerves_system_br, v1.35.2

This is a security and bug fix release.

  • Package updates

    • Erlang/OTP 29.1.2
    • Erlang/OTP 28.5.0.8
    • Erlang/OTP 27.3.4.19

  • Fixes

    • Stop using LD_LIBRARY_PATH for host toolchain binaries when building on Linux with Buildroot. This fixes ld.so warnings on distros that enable the warnings like Arch, and it’s also a good thing to do. (@CJRChang)
nerves_system_br, v1.35.3

This is an important security and bug fix release. Among other changes it has the eMudhra ca-certificate package update .

  • Package updates

    • Buildroot 2026.08.1

  • Changes

    • Include nerves-env.cmake in artifacts. This fixes issues with Hex packages that use CMake to build their native code.
    • If not using nerves-config to automatically select required Nerves system packages, don’t require the rngd acknowledgment.
    • Add nerves2-config for automatically selecting required Nerves 2 system packages. Importantly, this removes the SquashFS rootfs from the Nerves system artifact and will break compatibility with Nerves 1. This is unrelated to using SquashFS root filesystems on your target. There’s no urgency to switch your Nerves system to this unless you need to reduce the artifact size.

circuits_uart, v1.7.0

IMPORTANT: This release fixes a confusing issue where invalid data bit, stop bit, or parity settings were silently ignored. Because of this, upgrading to this version could break working code. The specific case it fixes was found when using circuits_uart with pseudo-terminals created by socat. The correct setting for these is 8 databits, no parity, and 1 stop bit which is the default.

  • Fixes
    • Return {:error, :einval} on Linux and macOS when opening or configuring a port if the driver doesn’t apply the requested data bits, stop bits, or parity.
    • Fix a build dependency race when running parallel make.

circuits_spi, v2.2.0

  • Backwards incompatible change

    • Change the Linux backend’s default :delay_us from 10 to 0 microseconds. Pass delay_us: 10 to Circuits.SPI.open/2 to preserve the previous post-transfer delay before chip select is deasserted.   • Changes

    • Don’t apply :delay_us on intermediate transfers when writing more than the low level max transfer size. The final transfer segment applies the delay.
    • Fix C compilation issue when passing MAKEFLAGS=j<n> when n > 1.

Got questions?

Trouble-shooting is best done on the Nerves Forum over at Elixir's Forum. But if you have big-picture questions you would like to ask around Nerves, feel free to send them in and we might just have ourselves a column here.

Participating in the community

The Nerves community is found wherever Elixirists gather. But our primary focus is the Nerves Discord . You are very welcome to join us there.

Also fine to throw questions into the Elixir Forum's Nerves section .

How you can help Nerves

Contribute in the way that works for you:

  • Send corrections or improvements for documentation wherever it fails to help you.
  • Write about Nerves, give talks about Nerves. Make videos about Nerves. It is all good.
  • Write or port a new hardware library and include it in the Elixir Circuits collection .
  • Get in touch about taking over maintenance duties for some libraries, we might be able to provide you hardware.
  • Apply for an EEF stipend on something Nerves-related and build it. We can help if you have questions about this.

Finally, if you have questions about the newsletter or want to suggest something you can simply respond to this email.
