Nerves sets an AI policy

August 20, 2026 • Issue 114

maintaining a trustworthy fundament

No images? Click here

Nerves has an AI policy now

This was established through discussion in the core team around many, many .. many instances of our use of LLMs, drive-by contributions via LLMs, thoughtful contributions via LLMs and everything we know so far. This is the new policy . There is a copy in every official nerves repo to hopefully encourage agents to surface this as well as help people find it.

"Contributions are made by people, not tools."

The core team have all tried LLMs to various extents and dialed up and dialed back their usage as we've seen where it works and what it does for us. The reason we land on having a person do all the textual communication around a contribution is multifaceted. Primarily it is about building trust with contributors and hopefully having a long-term exchange.

"Pull request titles, descriptions, issue reports, commit messages, and review discussions should be written by the contributor. Using AI to translate or tighten your own writing is fine. Using it to write in your place is not."

Friction is kind of the point. Unfortunately it is completely possible for one enthusiastic well-meaning individual (such as myself), with the help of some agents, to completely saturate the bandwidth of the Nerves core team in terms of review and feedback. And the review, the feedback and the consideration are all very important in keeping Nerves a solid foundation to build on top of. The more relied upon a piece of Nerves is the more seriously it is taken.

If you feel like this might be in response to something you specifically did. No. You are not in trouble. This is a very broad thing and we needed to make decisions to guide ourselves and others, and apparently their agents, on what we think will work.

We see plenty of AI-driven development around Nerves and we see people achieve things that would have been baffling in complexity a year or two ago. People push their products to new heights or they create new libraries and frameworks (see Damir's work on Emerge & Solve ) that simply wouldn't have been feasible without LLMs. We are not inherently against it. We are also well aware of the many good reasons to be concerned about the AI hype. The project doesn't take a hard stance in that because you'll do what you do.

We set the contribution policy to ensure we can keep Nerves as a very stable foundation. Feel free to reach out with any questions, notes or concerns. Best place for discussion is the Discord but you can respond to this email to reach me specifically.

    circuits_spi, v2.1.0

    • New features

      • Transfers greater than the max transfer size are supported. They’re automatically segmented in the NIF. While you can still segment in Elixir, allowing Circuits.SPI to do it is faster.       • Add more info to Circuits.SPI.info/0

    circuits_gpio, v2.3.0

    This is a larger than usual feature update. While it is unexpected to be backwards incompatible, please review the compatibility notes below just in case.

    • New features

      • Added Circuits.GPIO.subscribe/2 as an alternative to set_interrupts/3. This function simplifies matching of GPIO change notifications and is extensible. It is required for listening to changes for multi-GPIO handles.
      • Support opening multiple GPIOs using one handle. This allows backends to read and write a set of GPIOs atomically. It’s supported on Linux for GPIOs in the same hardware bank.
      • Support passing GPIO handles to Circuits.GPIO.status/1
      • Added Circuits.GPIO.force_close/1 to close open handles without knowing the handle. This is especially useful when working at the IEx prompt or Livebook and you’ve lost the handle. If the handle is used, it will raise an exception.
      • Added an on_busy: :take_over option to Circuits.GPIO.open/3 to automatically close any other open handles for the specified GPIO or GPIOs. This is not the default. It can be used in GenServer :init callbacks to avoid a race with the Erlang GC for cleaning up handles from a previous failure.

    • Backwards incompatible changes

      • Circuits.GPIO.write/2 no longer treats all non-zero values as 1. Even though the typespec for write/2 restricted values to 0 and 1, the implementation was forgiving. This is no longer the case.
      • Previously unknown errno values were reported as {:errno, n}. They’re now reported as their lowercase errno names using Erlang’s built-in errno conversion.

    Got questions?

    Trouble-shooting is best done on the Nerves Forum over at Elixir's Forum. But if you have big-picture questions you would like to ask around Nerves, feel free to send them in and we might just have ourselves a column here.

    Participating in the community

    The Nerves community is found wherever Elixirists gather. But our primary focus is the Nerves Discord . You are very welcome to join us there.

    Also fine to throw questions into the Elixir Forum's Nerves section .

    How you can help Nerves

    Contribute in the way that works for you:

    • Send corrections or improvements for documentation wherever it fails to help you.
    • Write about Nerves, give talks about Nerves. Make videos about Nerves. It is all good.
    • Write or port a new hardware library and include it in the Elixir Circuits collection .
    • Get in touch about taking over maintenance duties for some libraries, we might be able to provide you hardware.
    • Apply for an EEF stipend on something Nerves-related and build it. We can help if you have questions about this.

    Finally, if you have questions about the newsletter or want to suggest something you can simply respond to this email.
